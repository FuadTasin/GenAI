# The Road Beyond the Horizon

I walked one morning where the road
Ran quietly beneath the sky,
And carried every dream I owned
Beyond the hills I could not spy.
The world was waking, soft and slow,
The fields were dressed in drops of rain,
And somewhere far, a river flowed
Like silver through the waking plain.

I did not know where I would go,
Nor what the distant roads would bring;
I only knew I had to leave
The shelter of familiar things.
Behind me stood the little town,
The window lights, the garden wall,
The places where I learned to dream,
The hands that caught me when I'd fall.

The road was long, the morning wide,
And every mile revealed a view:
A mountain wearing clouds of white,
A meadow bright with drops of dew,
A lonely tree beside a stream,
A flock of birds across the blue,
And every sight became a part
Of all the things I never knew.

At noon the sun was fierce and high,
The road grew warm beneath my feet;
The distant mountains seemed to move,
Their shadows stretching in the heat.
I stopped beside a weathered stone
And watched the clouds go drifting past,
And wondered how the smallest things
Could make a moment seem to last.

Then evening came with amber light,
And painted gold across the land;
The wind became a softer voice,
The sky grew quiet, calm, and grand.
I heard the crickets start their song,
I saw the first star softly rise,
And felt a strange and peaceful truth:
There is no end beneath the skies.

Some nights are dark, and some are cold,
Some roads are steep and hard to climb;
There will be doors that will not open,
There will be losses, pain, and time.
There will be moments when the heart
Will question every step it made,
And ask if turning back would be
A safer choice beneath the shade.

But still the road will wait ahead,
A line of dust beneath the sun;
And though the journey may be hard,
The journey means the heart has begun.
For every mountain crossed in fear,
For every river passed alone,
Will leave a little strength behind,
A little courage of your own.

I met a traveler on the way,
An old man walking with a staff;
He smiled at me and said, “My friend,
The road is not a thing to master.
You cannot force the sky to clear,
Nor make the seasons hurry on;
You learn to walk through falling rain
And trust that night will meet the dawn.”

I asked him where the road would end.
He laughed and pointed to the west.
“The road will end,” he said, “one day,
But you should not make that your quest.
A journey is not measured by
The place where tired feet finally rest;
It lives in every step you take,
In every trial and every test.”

So I continued through the night,
Beneath a sky of countless stars;
I thought about the distant moon,
The quiet fields, the sleeping farms.
I thought of those I'd left behind,
And all the words I failed to say;
I wished them peace, I wished them light,
And carried all their love away.

By morning, I had reached a hill
That overlooked a shining sea.
The waves were moving far below,
And gulls were calling wild and free.
The sun appeared beyond the clouds,
And turned the ocean into fire;
I stood there silent for a while,
Amazed by all that I could see.

Then something changed inside my heart.
The world was not the same as before.
I understood that every step
Had brought me closer to this shore.
The fear I carried seemed so small,
The doubts no longer held their weight;
I had been searching for a place,
But found myself along the way.

Years passed, and many roads I walked.
Some led through cities full of light;
Some wandered through forgotten woods,
Some disappeared into the night.
I learned that happiness can hide
Inside a cup of morning tea,
Inside a friend's familiar laugh,
Inside a moment of being free.

I learned that dreams are fragile things,
But fragile things can still survive;
A seed can wait beneath the earth
Through winter and return alive.
A broken heart can learn to beat,
A tired soul can rise again;
The darkest clouds can lose their hold,
And flowers bloom after the rain.

I learned that people come and go,
Like travelers upon a train;
Some stay for years beside your side,
Some leave and never come again.
But every person leaves a mark,
A sentence in the story's page;
Some teach us how to love the world,
And some teach wisdom through their absence.

I learned that time is not a thief,
Though sometimes it may feel that way;
It takes the things we cannot keep,
But gives us something every day.
It gives us memories like stars,
It gives us lessons born from tears,
And slowly turns our yesterday
Into the wisdom of our years.

And now, when I look back along
The winding road that brought me here,
I see the child I used to be,
Still walking somewhere, without fear.
He carries dreams inside his hands,
He asks the world what lies ahead;
He does not know the road is long,
But keeps on walking anyway.

I wish I could go back to him
And tell him not to fear the rain,
Not every loss will break his heart,
Not every goodbye means pain.
I would tell him to hold on tight
To kindness, wonder, hope, and grace;
For life is not a perfect road,
But still a beautiful place.

I would tell him that courage means
Not never feeling scared at all;
It means you take another step
When fear is telling you to fall.
It means you stand when storms arrive,
And when you cannot stand, you rest;
Then rise again when morning comes,
And give tomorrow your best.

The road still stretches on ahead,
Beyond the forests, fields, and seas;
Beyond the places I have known,
Beyond the old familiar trees.
I cannot see where it will lead,
And perhaps I never will;
But there is beauty in the unknown,
And wonder waiting past the hill.

So if you find yourself one day
Alone upon a winding road,
With heavy thoughts and tired feet,
And more than you can bear to hold,
Remember this: the sky is wide,
And even stars must face the night;
The darkest hour cannot remain
When morning carries back the light.

Keep walking, even slowly then.
Keep dreaming, even if dreams change.
Keep loving, even after loss.
Keep growing through the fear and strange.
For life is not a single song,
But thousands woven into one;
Some verses filled with rain and grief,
Some bright with laughter, stars, and sun.

And when at last the journey ends,
When you look back across the years,
May you remember not the roads
That disappeared beneath your fears,
But all the moments that were real:
The hands you held, the skies you saw,
The times you chose to carry on,
The love that taught your heart to soar.

For every road has something more
Than where it leads beneath the sky;
It gives us stories, gives us scars,
And teaches us the reason why
We were not made to simply stay
Inside the places that we know.
We were made to walk, to learn, to love,
And find ourselves as we go.

So I will walk beyond the hill,
Where morning waits beyond the blue.
I do not know what waits for me,
But I will meet it when I do.
With open hands and hopeful eyes,
With all the lessons I have known,
I will keep walking toward the light,
And make the distant road my own.

And if tomorrow brings a storm,
I'll listen closely to the rain.
If sorrow comes, I'll let it pass,
And if I fall, I'll rise again.
For somewhere past the farthest hill,
Where all the roads and dreams unite,
There is a dawn I cannot see,
But I will walk toward it with light.

The road goes on.
The sky is wide.
The stars are waiting overhead.
And every step, however small,
Is one more page before the end.
So let the journey shape your heart,
Let every season teach and grow;
For life is not about the end—
It is the courage to keep going.
